# Thunder Gorge Trail

*A long, demanding route through a narrow gorge to a major waterfall, with several stream crossings.*

## Overview

Thunder Gorge Trail is the longest and most demanding hike covered here — a full-day route that follows a narrow river gorge upstream, crosses the stream several times, and finishes with a steep scramble to the base of a major waterfall. It combines distance, technical scrambling, and water crossings in a way no other trail in the area does.

## Details

- Difficulty: Hard
- Distance: 12.5 km
- Elevation Gain: 890 m
- Best For: Full-day adventures, waterfalls, experienced hikers comfortable with stream crossings
- Dogs Allowed: Not recommended (stream crossings, exposed scrambling)

## Water Crossings

There are three stream crossings along the route. The first two are usually manageable by rock-hopping in normal conditions, but the third — roughly 8 kilometers in — can require wading up to knee height during spring snowmelt. Water levels should be checked before setting out, particularly between April and June.

## Trail Notes

The gorge narrows considerably in its middle section, where the trail is squeezed between the rock wall and the river itself. This section floods first after heavy rain, so it's the first place to turn back if conditions deteriorate.

Past the gorge, the trail opens up briefly before the final approach to the waterfall, which involves a short but steep scramble over wet rock. The waterfall itself is significantly larger than Cascade Falls and drops into a deep plunge pool, though swimming is not advised due to strong currents near the base.

## Safety Notes

This is not a trail to underestimate. The combination of distance, stream crossings, and a wet scramble near the end means conditions matter more here than on almost any other route in the area. Turn back if water levels are high or weather is deteriorating — the gorge is a poor place to be caught in a flash flood.

## Best Season

Late summer through early fall offers the most reliable water levels and the driest scrambling conditions. Spring snowmelt (April-June) significantly raises the difficulty of the stream crossings and is best avoided by anyone without prior experience on this trail.
